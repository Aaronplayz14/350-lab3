1. What is a benefit (besides aliasing) of our `SharedPtr<T>` directly storing a “stored pointer” (we could always just
   access it through the control block, if we're not aliasing)?

   Every dereference avoids an indirection (a virtual call to managedAddress()): operator*, operator->, 
   and get() can return the stored pointer instantly rather than requesting the address from the control block.

2. What is a disadvantage of having the stored pointer (instead of going through the control block)?

   Because it replicates the ownership state, each copy, move, assignment, and reset must maintain the control block and the stored pointer in sync. 
   However, the two may not agree, adding bookkeeping per SharedPtr and costing an additional pointer.

3. From a system design/architecture perspective, what disadvantages are there to using shared pointers?

   Because there is no single owner in shared ownership, 
   an object's lifetime is determined by an opaque refcount rather than a clear ownership hierarchy, 
   which makes it more difficult to reason about lifetimes and dependencies. 
   It also makes it simple to unintentionally create ownership cycles that leak because their refcounts never reach zero.

4. From a performance perspective, what disadvantages there are to using shared pointers (say, versus a unique pointer
   or raw pointer)?

   In addition to the expense of a heap-allocated control block and an additional indirection to access it, 
   each copy and destruction results in a refcount increment/decrement , 
   which a unique pointer does not have.
